LOST-Device Restoration on Customer Rescue

When the customer of a device in LOST recharges and their connection is rescued, the device must return to DEPLOYED instead of staying stranded in LOST while actively serving a paying customer. Money is deliberately untouched — the loss penalty stays booked.

Author: Jaivin Prajapati (PM)  ·  Reviewer: Engineering  ·  Tier: Full

Quick Check

7-line triage. Filled after the body was completed.
What is changing?
A validated customer rescue now restores a LOST device to deployed; today the rescue is silently dropped and the device stays LOST forever.
What triggered it?
Netbox recovery lifecycle gap analysis, verified against production (2026-06-04): 3,124 devices currently sit in LOST; a rescue arriving for any of them has no effect today — the system would show a lost device serving a live, paying customer.
Blast radius
Device lifecycle at customer rescue; partner exposure view re-opens via existing mechanics. Actors: CSP partners, ops/inventory. Pickup tasks are NOT touched by this change.
Reversible?
Yes — the broadened rescue eligibility can revert to today's behaviour; restored devices keep their full audit history.
Money / PII / migration?
Money: adjacent but explicitly unchanged — ₹200 loss penalty stays booked, no reversal (A5-3, A8). PII: No. Migration: No.
OS impact / sign-off?
Asset Custody OS — Yes, amendment signed off (Received · 2026-06-04). All other OSes declared No. All signed off: Y.
Tier
Full — a lifecycle state machine is touched and money is adjacent. (Lead PM: Jaivin Prajapati)

A2. Why This Change WHAT

QuestionAnswer
What's wrong with the current behaviour?When the customer of a LOST device recharges, the connection comes back live and paying — but the rescue has no effect on the device, which stays LOST permanently. There is no path back to deployed from LOST. Production verification (2026-06-04): 3,124 devices currently sit in LOST (87 via partner loss declarations, the remainder largely migration-era records); any of their customers recharging today produces a live connection served by a device the business has written off as lost.
Who experiences it?Ops/inventory teams (asset truth is wrong — a "lost" device is in active service) and CSP partners (their device and exposure view contradicts reality; the device never re-enters their exposure).
Cost of leaving it as-is?Inventory and exposure figures distorted by live-but-LOST devices; every such case needs a manual correction; the distortion grows as rescues of lost devices accumulate against a 3,000+ LOST stock.

A3. Current Behaviour WHAT

Trigger / conditionObservable behaviour today
Device awaiting recovery from the customer; the customer recharges and the connection is rescued (connection and partner validations pass)The device is restored to DEPLOYED; recovery tracking is cleared; the device re-enters the partner's exposure view; downstream consequences of the rescue fire (e.g. an open pickup task on the device is cancelled as "rescued").
Device already moved to LOST; the customer recharges and the connection is rescuedThe rescue has no effect on the device — it stays LOST permanently. The connection is live and paying; the ₹200 penalty booked at loss declaration remains booked.
Rescue arrives whose connection or partner does not match the device awaiting recoveryThe rescue is rejected; the device is unchanged.

A4. Desired Behaviour WHAT

Trigger / conditionDesired observable behaviour
Device awaiting recovery from the customer; the customer recharges and the connection is rescued (validations pass)Unchanged — restored to DEPLOYED exactly as today, with the same downstream consequences.
Device already moved to LOST; the customer recharges and the connection is rescued (connection and partner validations pass)The device is restored to DEPLOYED; recovery tracking is cleared to a fresh deployed baseline; the device re-enters the partner's exposure view; the same rescue outcome is announced as for today's restoration, so downstream consequences behave identically. The ₹200 loss penalty stays booked — no refund, no reversal.
Rescue arrives whose connection or partner does not match the device (whether awaiting recovery or LOST)The rescue is rejected; the device is unchanged — same gates, now applied to both eligible states.

A5. The Delta WHAT

What changesBeforeAfterWhy
1 · Rescue eligibility on customer rechargeOnly a device awaiting recovery from the customer is restored to deployed when its connection is rescued; a device in LOST is invisible to the rescue, which is silently dropped.A device in LOST is also restored to deployed on rescue, subject to the same connection and partner validation gates as today.The connection is live and paying again; the device record must follow operational reality.
2 · Device lifecycle exit from LOSTA LOST device can only move to written-off or recovery-hold; there is no path back to deployed.LOST may additionally exit to deployed, solely via a validated customer rescue.Enables the restoration in row 1; ships Patch 1 (T_LOSS_RECLAIM_DEPLOYED / INV-ACO-RL-02) of the approved Asset Custody OS amendment standalone.
3 · Loss penalty on restorationNot applicable — restoration from LOST is impossible.The ₹200 booked when the device went LOST remains booked; restoration triggers no refund or reversal, and neither compensation nor payment is involved.Penalty reversal is a separate, unshipped amendment; keeping money untouched bounds this change's risk.

A6. Must-Not-Change — Regression Guard GUARD

Adjacent behaviour that must not changeWhy it's in the blast radius
1 · Rescue of a device awaiting recovery from the customer (in-window) restores it exactly as today.The rescue handling is being extended with a second eligible state; the existing branch must stay byte-identical.
2 · The ₹200 penalty booking at the moment of loss declaration is unchanged in timing and amount.The change is adjacent to the penalty flow; restoring devices must not disturb how or when the penalty is booked.
3 · A written-off device is never restored by any rescue.Adding an exit from LOST must not leak an exit out of the true terminal state.
4 · A rescue with a mismatched connection or partner is rejected, for both eligible states.The validation gates are being reused on a new branch; rejection behaviour must hold everywhere.
5 · Downstream consequences of a rescue (pickup-task cancellation as "rescued", partner exposure re-opening) behave exactly as for today's restoration.The new path must announce the same rescue outcome as the existing path; any difference would fork downstream behaviour.

A7. Rules, Invariants & Edge Cases Affected WHAT

A7.1 Rules & invariants (must always hold)

Rule / invariantStatement
RULE — gated restoration onlyA LOST device becomes deployed only via a customer rescue that passes the same connection and partner validation as today's rescue.
INV — no live-and-lostOnce a customer rescue is processed successfully, no device is simultaneously LOST and bound to a live, paying connection.
INV — money immutability on restorationRestoration from loss changes no money: the loss penalty remains booked, and neither compensation nor payment activity is triggered at any point.
INV — terminal stays terminalA written-off device never leaves the written-off state.

A7.2 Edge cases

Edge / conditionIntended business outcome
1 · Duplicate customer rescue (same notification delivered twice)No double effect — device stays deployed; nothing re-fires.
2 · Rescue arrives for a written-off deviceRejected; device stays written-off (restoration applies to LOST only).
3 · A device restored from LOST is later declared lost againA fresh loss cycle applies under existing, unchanged rules — including a new penalty booking.
4 · Rescue for a connection whose LOST device has no recorded connection linkage (e.g. migration-era import with no last-connection on record)The device cannot be matched; behaviour is identical to today (no restoration). Backfilling linkage for imported records is out of scope.
5 · A pickup task is still open on the LOST device when it is rescuedThe task follows today's existing rescue consequence — cancelled as "rescued". No new task behaviour is introduced by this change.

B5. Acceptance Criteria — Verification Layer HOW

Business logic

LOGIC_01 (business logic · happy): Rescue restores a LOST device — A5-1, A5-2, RULE gated restoration
Given a device in LOST whose customer recharges, with matching connection and partner
When the connection rescue is processed
Then the device is restored to DEPLOYED with recovery tracking cleared
And the same rescue outcome is announced as for today's restoration from awaiting-recovery
LOGIC_02 (business logic · negative): Gate mismatch is rejected — A5-1, RULE gated restoration
Given a device in LOST
When a rescue arrives whose connection or partner does not match the device's binding
Then the rescue is rejected
And the device stays LOST
LOGIC_03 (business logic · negative): Restoration never refunds — A5-3
Given a device in LOST with the ₹200 penalty booked
When the device is restored to DEPLOYED via a valid rescue
Then no refund or reversal occurs
And no compensation or payment activity is triggered

Computation & calculation

COMPUTE_01 (computation · normal): Restoration resets the deployed baseline
Given a device in LOST with accumulated recovery-tracking values
When it is restored to DEPLOYED via a valid rescue
Then recovery tracking is cleared to a fresh deployed baseline — idle time restarts from zero and no carrying charge is active
And the baseline is identical to that of a device restored from awaiting-recovery today
COMPUTE_02 (computation · boundary): Long-duration loss does not accrue or prorate
Given a device that has been LOST for an extended period (e.g. months) before its customer recharges
When it is restored to DEPLOYED
Then exactly one ₹200 penalty remains booked — no additional accrual, proration, or recomputation for the time spent in LOST
And idle tracking still restarts from zero regardless of the duration

Invariants

INVARIANT_01 (from A7.1): Money immutability on restoration
Given any device restored from LOST, across all rescue paths including duplicates
Then the loss penalty remains booked at all times
And at no point is any compensation or payment activity triggered by the restoration
INVARIANT_02 (from A7.1): Terminal stays terminal
Given any written-off device at any moment, whatever events arrive — including a customer rescue
Then the device remains written-off
And no path introduced by this change exits the written-off state
INVARIANT_03 (from A7.1): No live-and-lost
Given any device whose customer rescue has been processed successfully
Then the device is not in LOST while bound to a live, paying connection
And this holds for every rescue processed after this change ships

Use & edge cases

EDGE_01 (edge · A7.2-1): Duplicate rescue
Given a device already restored to DEPLOYED by a rescue
When the same rescue is delivered again
Then the device stays DEPLOYED and nothing re-fires
EDGE_02 (edge · A7.2-2): Rescue for a written-off device
Given a written-off device
When a customer rescue arrives for its last connection
Then the rescue is rejected and the device stays written-off
EDGE_03 (edge · A7.2-3): Re-loss after restoration
Given a device previously restored from LOST and later declared lost again
When the new loss cycle runs
Then existing rules apply afresh — including a new penalty booking
EDGE_04 (edge · A7.2-4): LOST device with no connection linkage
Given a LOST device with no recorded last connection (e.g. a migration-era import)
When a rescue arrives for the connection its customer recharges
Then the device is not matched and behaviour is identical to today — no restoration, no error introduced by this change
EDGE_05 (edge · A7.2-5): Open pickup task on the rescued device
Given a LOST device that still has an open pickup task
When the device is restored to DEPLOYED via a valid rescue
Then the task is cancelled as "rescued" exactly as it would be for today's restoration
And no new task behaviour is introduced

Regression (from A6)

REGRESSION_01 (guard · A6-1): In-window rescue unchanged
Given a device awaiting recovery from the customer (in-window)
When its customer's connection is rescued with matching connection and partner
Then restoration to DEPLOYED behaves exactly as before this change
REGRESSION_02 (guard · A6-2): Penalty booking unchanged
Given a device being declared LOST
When the loss is processed
Then the ₹200 penalty is booked at the same moment and amount as before this change
REGRESSION_03 (guard · A6-3): Written-off never restored
Given a written-off device
When any rescue arrives
Then the device is not restored — written-off behaviour is exactly as before this change
REGRESSION_04 (guard · A6-4): Validation gates still reject
Given a device awaiting recovery from the customer
When a rescue arrives with a mismatched connection or partner
Then it is rejected exactly as before this change — the broadened eligibility has not weakened the gates
REGRESSION_05 (guard · A6-5): Downstream rescue consequences unchanged
Given a device restored via either eligible state
When the restoration completes
Then the announced rescue outcome is identical in kind to today's — pickup-task cancellation as "rescued" and partner exposure re-opening behave exactly as before this change
