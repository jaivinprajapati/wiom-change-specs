CSP Netbox Swap — Diagnosis-Driven Device Replacement

Backend specification for the netbox swap flow, described through the CSP's journey. The CSP enters old + new device on a ticket; gate checks return pass/fail (nothing executes); the CSP physically swaps the hardware and confirms, which creates the swap record and starts a 20-minute timer. ACS then owns the swap end-to-end — a provisional swap via the Remote API, then verification by the common Validation Layer (first check automatic at 10 min, then CSP-driven retries 2 min apart). On confirmation ACS commits the final custody change and tells SRS to resolve the ticket; otherwise the swap expires or is cancelled and the provisional shift is reversed. A device can be in only one swap at a time; the ticket's Resolve CTA is blocked while a swap is in progress; state is server-authoritative. SRS fundamentals are a separate amendment, not part of this spec. Per author directive, exact technical references are carried inline — a deliberate deviation from the template's default scope fence.

Author: Jaivin Prajapati  ·  Reviewer: Engineering  ·  Tier: Full

What is changing?
A net-new CSP swap journey owned by ACS: gate-check → physical-swap confirmation (creates the record + a 20-min timer) → ACS provisional swap (Remote API) → Validation Layer (auto check at 10 min, then CSP retries 2 min apart) → commit + resolve on confirm, else expire/cancel + reverse. Product decision: today's swaps run outside the OS with no verifiable record; the OS-governed flow is unreachable (no diagnosis path) and, even if it ran, internet would not actually shift to the new device.

Reversible?
Feature: Yes — disabling the swap CTA stops all swaps. A committed swap: No un-swap. A pre-commit (provisional) swap: reversed on expire/cancel.

A2. Why This Change WHAT

QuestionAnswer
What's wrong with the current behaviour?When a CSP/technician diagnoses a faulty netbox, there is no in-system way to swap it: the OS-governed swap flow exists in the backend but is unreachable (no path feeds it a diagnosis, so its entry guards can never pass). And even if it executed, custody records would change while actual internet delivery — driven by the old-app Remote and Inventory services — would not, leaving the customer offline with a "completed" swap.
Who experiences it?Customers with device-fault internet issues (no resolution, or a paper resolution with no internet); CSPs and technicians (no legitimate swap path); Wiom inventory & finance (swaps happen off-books and cannot be reconciled).
Cost of leaving it as-is?Every swap remains an unaudited device movement: unrecoverable devices, unreconcilable inventory, customer downtime, and a custody ledger that can drift from connectivity reality.

Confirmed with author 2026-06-06 (re-scoped to swap-only in v0.2). Observable behaviour of the slice being changed. Technical references inline per author directive.
Trigger / conditionObservable behaviour today
CSP works a RESTORE task and concludes the device is faultyThere is no way to tell the system. The diagnosis fields exist on the task record (complaint_tasks.diagnosis_outcome, complaint_tasks.diagnosis_fault_type) but nothing feeds them — so the swap entry guards (G8 "diagnosis complete", G9 "device fault type") can never pass, and the swap flow is unreachable in practice.

Custody says "swapped"; connectivity says otherwise. A4. Desired Behaviour WHAT

The flow described as the CSP experiences it, end to end. The backend behaviour behind each step is in A5; the screen-level detail is in B4.
Step / triggerDesired observable behaviour
1–2. CSP opens an SRS ticket and taps Netbox Swap (a CTA separate from Resolve Ticket)The swap journey opens. The Resolve CTA and the swap CTA are distinct actions.
3. CSP enters old + new netbox details (and the device-fault diagnosis + resolution type)SRS persists the diagnosis on the task. The request carries old_device_id, new_device_id, diagnosis_fault_type, diagnosis_outcome, resolution_type = DEVICE_REPLACED.
4. Backend runs gate checks — only the pre-swap "is this allowed?" validationSRS guards (G7–G11) + device eligibility (G1–G5, read-only via ACS) + the new one-swap-per-device check run and return a single pass/fail. Nothing is executed and no swap record exists yet. On fail, the CSP sees the specific reason.
5. On pass, CSP is told "swapping is allowed — now physically swap the devices" and taps "physical swap done"This is the trigger to begin. ACS creates a swap record (old device, new device, csp id, SRS id, time) and a 20-minute exhaust timer starts. Both devices are reserved (no other swap can use them).
6. Swap moves to ACS, which owns it end-to-endThe FE shows a countdown and asks the CSP to wait. From here ACS runs provisional → validate → commit and reports only the outcome to SRS.
7A. Provisional swap (ACS → Remote API)Success: nothing shown; proceed to validation. Failure (any reason): the swap is ABORTED, the CSP sees an error and is asked to try again — restarting from step 5 (a fresh record + timer; gates already passed).
7B. Validation — first check automatic, retries UI-drivenThe first VL check fires automatically at T0 + 10 min (giving the new device time to come up). VL confirms = new device pinged ≥1 in the last 10 min AND old device not pinging. Confirmed on a check → ACS commits the custody swap, shows the success screen, and tells SRS to resolve the ticket. Not confirmed → error shown; after a 2-minute cooldown a Retry CTA appears; on tap, ACS calls VL again. The CSP may keep retrying until the 20-minute exhaust cap. They may also cancel at any time.
7B (terminal). Cap hit or cancelledOn the 20-min cap → EXPIRED; on cancel → CANCELLED. In both, the provisional swap is reversed, the CSP is returned to the SRS page and shown the swap-back message, and they can start a fresh swap on the same ticket from scratch.
7C. CSP navigates away (back button / app close) during the waitThe backend keeps running. If the swap completes while they're elsewhere, the ticket is resolved (no real-time FE removal needed). If it expires, the ticket is unchanged and they can restart fresh. If it's still in progress, returning to the ticket shows the live timer.
8. While a swap is in progressBoth devices are locked out of any other swap. The ticket's Resolve CTA is blocked — the ticket cannot be resolved any other way until the swap reaches a terminal state. A COMPLETED swap resolves it; EXPIRED/CANCELLED/ABORTED leave it open.

A7. Rules, Invariants & Edge Cases Affected WHAT

A7.1 Rules & invariants (must always hold)

#Rule / invariantStatement
I1INV — complaint anchoringEvery swap carries a complaint_id in ASSIGNED or ESCALATED at initiation. A swap without an open ticket is impossible.
I2INV — custody atomicityOn commit, both devices transition or neither. No partial custody outcome is ever observable.
I3INV — single bindingAt most one device bound to a connection at any moment; after a committed swap, exactly the new device is bound.
I4INV — diagnosis gateNo swap proceeds without a persisted device fault type {DEVICE_HARDWARE, DEVICE_SOFTWARE, DEVICE_CONNECTIVITY}. Latest diagnosis governs.
I5INV — physical-confirm before provisioningNo swap record is created and no provisioning starts until the CSP confirms the physical swap.
I6INV — verify-before-commitACS commits custody only after a VL check returns CONFIRMED. No confirmation → no custody change; the provisional swap is reversed on a terminal non-confirmation.
I7INV — resolution-type consistencyEvery swap carries and every committed swap records resolution_type = DEVICE_REPLACED.
I8INV — no false closureOnly a COMPLETED swap resolves the ticket. EXPIRED, CANCELLED, and ABORTED never resolve it.
I9INV — ticket lock (CSP scope)While a swap is in progress (REQUESTED/PROVISIONAL), the ticket's Resolve CTA and other CSP-initiated mutating actions are blocked. System-driven transitions are not blocked. The lock releases only on a terminal swap state.
I10INV — swap-log completenessEvery swap attempt has a swap-table record carrying its lifecycle to a terminal state (COMPLETED/EXPIRED/CANCELLED/ABORTED/REVERSAL_FAILED). No Remote or custody action without a log entry.
I11INV — one swap per deviceA device is part of at most one in-progress swap. Both devices are atomically reserved at record creation; a device in an in-progress swap fails the gate for any other swap.
I12INV — bounded lifetimeEvery swap request reaches a terminal state within the 20-minute exhaust cap. No swap stays in progress indefinitely.
R1RULE — unlimited repeat swapsAfter any terminal state, the CSP may start a fresh swap on the same ticket; each is a new record. No cap on attempts in v1.
R2RULE — swap-as-closureA COMPLETED swap resolves the ticket; there is no separate resolution submission for a swap-resolved ticket.
R3RULE — duplicate-submission safetyDuplicate physical-confirm taps create one record (idempotent). A duplicate custody commit produces no second transition.
R4RULE — no un-swap of a committed swapOnce committed and the ticket resolved, custody is never reversed. Any later need is a fresh forward swap on a reopened ticket.

Exact validation & timer model (D6/D7)

Let T₀ = physical-swap confirmed (record created, timer starts). Provisional swap runs immediately after.

Per-check condition at evaluation time t, over the trailing 10-min window (t − 10 min, t]: new_up = new device ≥ 1 ping; old_down = old device 0 pings; CONFIRM = both.

Check schedule:
· Check 1 — automatic at T₀ + 10 min.
· If not CONFIRM → error; after a 2-min cooldown the Retry CTA is enabled.
· Each subsequent check is CSP-triggered (Retry), at least 2 min after the previous check.
· The CSP may cancel at any time.

Terminal: COMPLETED on the first CONFIRM (→ custody commit → ticket resolved); EXPIRED at T₀ + 20 min with no CONFIRM (→ reverse); CANCELLED on cancel (→ reverse). EXPIRED/CANCELLED reverse the provisional swap; a failed reverse → REVERSAL_FAILED + ops.

F. Future Scope — Noted, Not Specced Out of scope for this version

Items intentionally parked. Recorded so the intent isn't lost, but explicitly not part of this build.

F1. Closing a Pickup Ticket (PUT) by swapping a device

AspectDetail
The gap / scenarioA frustrated customer raises a PUT (intent to leave + return the device). The CSP visits, diagnoses on site, and saves the customer by giving a new device. Today a PUT can only close via recharge or pickup — not a save-via-swap.
The proposalSwapping as a third PUT-closure path, alongside recharge and pickup.
Why deferredRipples across Exit OS, CL OS, Asset Custody OS, and SR OS; breaks invariant I1 (a swap needs an open complaint). Its own initiative.
StatusNoted only.

F2. Non-swap concurrency between physical-confirm and commit

AspectDetail
The gapOne-swap-per-device (I11) stops two swaps fighting over a device. But a device's custody state could still change via a non-swap process during the ~20-min window; ACS re-reads state at commit but holds no exclusive cross-process lock.
Why deferredA full reservation across all OSes is larger than this effort. Accepted as a known limitation; the commit re-read is the safeguard.
StatusNoted only.

F3. Validation beyond a ping (true service check)

AspectDetail
The gapv1 treats a ping as proof of internet; a device can ping while the customer still has no usable service, and a ping proves the device is alive, not that it serves this connection.
Why deferredDeeper checks (session/auth/throughput/connection-binding) are a future VL enhancement; the validation outcome flag helps spot patterns that justify them.
StatusNoted only.
