Wiom · Change Spec · v0.13 · PM deliverable

CSP Netbox Swap — Diagnosis-Driven Device Replacement

Backend specification for the netbox swap flow, described through the CSP's journey. Gate checks return pass/fail (nothing executes); the CSP physically swaps the hardware and configures the new device on the ISP portal, then confirms — creating the swap record and a 20-minute timer. ACS owns the swap, forward-only: a connectivity swap via the Remote API, then verification by the common Validation Layer (first check automatic at 10 min, then immediate CSP retries; a self-confirm appears after 3 failed retries). The swap always completes — validated, self-confirmed, or auto-confirmed at the 20-minute cap — and ACS commits custody and resolves the ticket. There is no reversal or cancel. A device/ticket can be in only one swap at a time; Resolve is replaced by "continue swap" during a swap; state is server-authoritative and resumable. SRS fundamentals are a separate amendment, not part of this spec. Exact technical references are carried inline per author directive.

Author: Jaivin Prajapati  ·  Reviewer: Engineering  ·  Tier: Full

Reversible?
Feature: Yes — disabling the swap CTA stops all swaps. An individual swap: No — forward-only by design; once the physical handoff is confirmed it always completes.

A2. Why This Change WHAT

QuestionAnswer
What's wrong with the current behaviour?When a CSP/technician diagnoses a faulty netbox, there is no in-system way to swap it: the OS-governed swap flow exists in the backend but is unreachable (no path feeds it a diagnosis, so its entry guards can never pass). And even if it executed, custody records would change while actual internet delivery — driven by the old-app Remote and Inventory services — would not, leaving the customer offline with a "completed" swap.
Who experiences it?Customers with device-fault internet issues (no resolution, or a paper resolution with no internet); CSPs and technicians (no legitimate swap path); Wiom inventory & finance (swaps happen off-books and cannot be reconciled).
Cost of leaving it as-is?Every swap remains an unaudited device movement: unrecoverable devices, unreconcilable inventory, customer downtime, and a custody ledger that can drift from connectivity reality.

Confirmed with author 2026-06-06 (re-scoped to swap-only in v0.2). Observable behaviour of the slice being changed. Technical references inline per author directive.
Trigger / conditionObservable behaviour today
CSP works a RESTORE task and concludes the device is faultyThere is no way to tell the system. A swap is submitted (POST /tasks/{taskId}/device-swap with old_device_id, new_device_id)SRS validates G6–G11 — including G6, a connection-state query to CL OS — and emits DEVICE_SWAP. ACS validates G1–G5 and immediately executes the atomic custody transition (old device DEPLOYED → RETRIEVAL_PENDING, new device CUSTODIED/IDLE → DEPLOYED), then emits DEVICE_SWAP_COMPLETED. No external system is consulted before execution.
The swap completesNothing informs the old-app stack (Remote service, Inventory service) — actual internet delivery does not shift to the new device. Custody says "swapped"; connectivity says otherwise. On receiving DEVICE_SWAP_COMPLETED, SRS records the swap on the complaint (complaints.device_swap_old_device_id, device_swap_new_device_id, device_swap_initiated_at, device_swap_completed_at, resolution_type = DEVICE_REPLACED) and auto-moves the complaint to PENDING_VERIFICATION — swap completion is treated as the resolution action itself.
Verification window runsVerification is SRS-internal today (telemetry check always inconclusive in v1; customer confirmation or timeout resolves; denial re-escalates). A common verification service — which will verify all service tickets in general, not just swaps — is being built in parallel and is not yet the verifier.
CSP attempts another swap on the same complaintAllowed for the time being — guards re-evaluate and a second swap proceeds if they pass. Nothing additional governs it.
Guard failure / no completionAny guard failure → rejection with reason (DEVICE_SWAP_REJECTED), complaint unchanged. No completion within 120 s (P_SWAP_COMPLETION_TIMEOUT) → ops flag, complaint stays open, late completion still processed.

A4. Desired Behaviour WHAT

The CSP journey, end to end. The swap is forward-only — once the physical swap is confirmed it always completes (validated, self-confirmed, or auto-confirmed); there is no reversal or cancel. Backend behaviour is in A5; screens in B4.
Step / triggerDesired observable behaviour
1–2. CSP opens an SRS ticket and taps Netbox Swap (separate from Resolve)The swap journey opens. While a swap is in progress, the ticket's Resolve action is replaced by a "continue swap" entry.
3. CSP records the diagnosis and the new device (scan/enter)SRS persists the diagnosis. Request carries old_device_id, new_device_id, diagnosis_fault_type, diagnosis_outcome, resolution_type = DEVICE_REPLACED.
4. Gate check (pre-swap "is this allowed?")SRS guards (G7–G11) + device eligibility (G1–G5) + one-swap-per-device + one-swap-per-ticket → single pass/fail. Nothing executes, no record yet. Fail → specific reason.
5. On pass → physical-handoff screen, three items(A) complete the physical swap + plug in the new device · (B) open the [config link] to configure the new device on the ISP network · (C) confirm on the app once both are done. The config link is task-scoped (backend-supplied).
6. CSP taps "I've swapped the device" (A + B done)ACS creates the swap record (old, new, csp id, srs id, time) + reserves both devices + starts the 20-min timer. ACS performs the connectivity swap via the Remote API. The FE shows a countdown to the first verification.
7. Verification — first check automatic at 10 minAt T0 + 10 min ACS runs the first VL check (new device up in last 10 min AND old device down). Confirmed → swap completes (VL_VALIDATED), success screen, ticket resolved, "return the old box" reminder.
7-fail. Not verified → Retry (immediate)The CSP sees a not-verified screen with a Retry CTA (tappable immediately — no wait). Each retry re-runs the VL check on the same device. Retries are counted.
7-escalate. After 3 failed retries → self-confirmOnce P_SWAP_MAX_RETRIES (3) retries have failed, a self-confirm CTA appears: "I confirm the customer's internet is working even though Wiom can't validate it." Tapping it completes the swap (CSP_OVERRIDE) → ticket resolved.
7-cap. 20-min exhaust → backend auto-confirmsIf the swap hasn't completed by P_SWAP_EXHAUST_CAP (20 min), the backend auto-confirms it (AUTO_OVERRIDE): ACS commits custody and SRS resolves the ticket — server-side, regardless of which screen the CSP is on. (Prevents an in-progress swap drifting the device state between Remote and ACS indefinitely.)
8. Navigate away during the swapThe backend keeps running. The ticket shows "continue swap" to resume at the exact step (durable, survives app kill). If it completed/auto-completed while away → the ticket is resolved; the CSP just sees it resolved on return.
9. LockingBoth devices are locked out of any other swap; the ticket can't be resolved any other way while a swap is in progress. The lock covers CSP actions + concurrent swaps only — system-driven transitions are not blocked.

A7. Rules, Invariants & Edge Cases Affected WHAT

A7.1 Rules & invariants (must always hold)

#Rule / invariantStatement
I1INV — complaint anchoringEvery swap carries a complaint_id in ASSIGNED or ESCALATED at initiation.
I2INV — custody atomicityOn commit, both devices transition or neither; no partial outcome is observable.
I3INV — single bindingAt most one device bound to a connection; after a committed swap, exactly the new device.
I4INV — diagnosis gateNo swap proceeds without a persisted device fault type. Latest diagnosis governs.
I5INV — physical-confirm before provisioningNo record and no provisioning until the CSP confirms the physical handoff (swap + config done).
I6INV — commit only after validate-or-overrideACS commits custody only after a VL check passes, a CSP self-confirm, or the auto-confirm at the cap. No commit without one of these.
I7INV — forward-onlyThere is no reversal, cancel, or expire-with-undo. A swap, once the physical handoff is confirmed, always reaches COMMITTED (or an ops terminal). The Remote connectivity swap is never rolled back by the system.
I8INV — completion is recordedEvery COMMITTED swap records commit_reason ∈ {VL_VALIDATED, CSP_OVERRIDE, AUTO_OVERRIDE}; an override also records actor + time + attestation.
I9INV — ticket lock (CSP scope)While a swap is in progress, the ticket's Resolve and other CSP-initiated actions are blocked; system-driven transitions are not blocked. The lock releases on a terminal swap state.
I10INV — swap-log completenessEvery attempt has a swap-table record to a terminal state (COMMITTED / ABORTED / COMMIT_FAILED). No Remote or custody action without a record.
I11INV — one swap per device and per ticketA device is in at most one in-progress swap; a ticket has at most one in-progress swap. Devices reserved atomically at record creation. After a terminal state, a fresh swap on the same device/ticket is allowed.
I12INV — bounded lifetimeEvery swap completes within P_SWAP_EXHAUST_CAP (auto-confirm backstop). No swap stays in progress indefinitely.
R1RULE — self-confirm gateThe self-confirm CTA is offered only after P_SWAP_MAX_RETRIES (3) failed retries.
R2RULE — swap-as-closureA committed swap resolves the ticket; no separate resolution submission for a swap-resolved ticket.
R3RULE — duplicate-submission safetyDuplicate physical-confirm taps create one record; a duplicate custody commit produces no second transition.
R4RULE — no un-swap of a committed swapOnce committed and the ticket resolved, custody is never reversed; any later need is a fresh forward swap on a reopened ticket.

Validation & completion model

Let T₀ = physical-confirm (record + timer start; Remote swap runs immediately after). Per VL check at time t, over the trailing P_VL_PING_LOOKBACK window: new_up = new device ≥1 ping; old_down = old device 0 pings; CONFIRM = both.
· Check 1 automatic at T₀ + P_VL_FIRST_CHECK_DELAY (10 min).
· On fail → Retry immediately (CSP-driven); each retry re-runs the check; retry_count++.
· After P_SWAP_MAX_RETRIES (3) fails → self-confirm CTA available → COMMITTED (CSP_OVERRIDE).
· At T₀ + P_SWAP_EXHAUST_CAP (20 min) with no completion → backend auto-confirms → COMMITTED (AUTO_OVERRIDE).
· Any CONFIRM → COMMITTED (VL_VALIDATED). First terminal wins; later signals are idempotent no-ops.

F. Future Scope — Noted, Not Specced Out of scope for this version

Items intentionally parked. Recorded so the intent isn't lost, but explicitly not part of this build.

F1. Closing a Pickup Ticket (PUT) by swapping a device

AspectDetail
The gapA churning customer raises a PUT; the CSP saves them on site with a new device. Today a PUT can only close via recharge or pickup — not a save-via-swap.
Why deferredRipples across Exit OS, CL OS, Custody OS, SR OS; breaks I1 (a swap needs an open complaint). Its own initiative.

F2. Non-swap concurrency between confirm and commit

AspectDetail
The gapOne-swap-per-device/ticket stops concurrent swaps, but a device's custody state could change via a non-swap process during the window; ACS re-reads at commit but holds no exclusive cross-process lock.
Why deferredA full reservation across OSes is larger than this effort.

F3. Validation beyond a ping

AspectDetail
The gapv1 treats a ping as proof of internet; a device can ping while service is down, and a ping proves the device is alive, not serving this connection.
Why deferredDeeper checks (session/auth/throughput) are a future VL enhancement.

F4. Genuinely-faulty replacement device

AspectDetail
The scenarioThe internet genuinely isn't working and the new device is also faulty. The CSP still completes the swap forward (self-confirm or auto-confirm) — the system has no software un-swap — and then raises it to the Wiom ops team for next steps.
Why deferredReversal / refurb of a faulty replacement, and the ops-handoff workflow, are out of v1 scope. v1 only guarantees the swap is recorded forward; the ops follow-up is a separate path.
StatusNoted only.
