Wiom · Change Spec · v0.6 · PM deliverable

CSP Netbox Swap — Diagnosis-Driven Device Replacement

Backend specification for the netbox swap flow inside SRS: a swap initiates on an open service ticket with a device-fault diagnosis, executes through ACS only after the old-app services (Remote, Inventory) confirm the connectivity shift, and completes without resolving the ticket — resolution and verification then proceed through the normal flow, verified by the common verification service. Scope is strictly swap-related; SRS fundamentals (ticket-creation gating, complaint lifecycle automatics) are a separate amendment and are not part of this spec. Per author directive, exact technical references (API paths, event names, field names, DB columns) are carried inline — a deliberate deviation from the template's default scope fence.

Author: Jaivin Prajapati  ·  Reviewer: Engineering  ·  Tier: Full

Quick Check

7-line triage. Filled last, after the body.
What is changing?
A net-new swap flow: swap initiation carries the diagnosis fault type + resolution type; SRS orchestrates the old-app connectivity shift; the common Validation Layer confirms the swap physically happened (new device up + old device down) before ACS commits custody; a confirmed swap auto-closes the ticket; an unconfirmed swap is reversed; guard G6 removed.
What triggered it?
Product decision: today's swaps run outside the OS with no verifiable record; the OS-governed flow is unreachable (no diagnosis path) and, even if it ran, internet would not actually shift to the new device.
Blast radius
Swap initiation & guards (SRS), the old-app connectivity shift + reversal (Remote + Inventory), swap verification (common Validation Layer), custody execution (ACS — now validate-then-execute), ticket closure (SRS).
Reversible?
Feature: Yes — disabling swap initiation stops all swaps cleanly. A VL-confirmed (completed) swap: No un-swap. A pre-confirmation swap: reversed via the old-app shift on a non-confirmation.
Money / PII / migration?
N / N / N.
OS impact / sign-off?
SR OS (behaviour change — Varun) · Asset Custody OS (validate/execute split — Dhruv) · ESR (contract — Dhruv) · Remote + Inventory (forward+reverse contract — Rahul Nijhawan) · Validation Layer (swap rules — VL-owning PM TBD). All Pending.

A2. Why This Change WHAT

QuestionAnswer
What's wrong with the current behaviour?When a CSP/technician diagnoses a faulty netbox, there is no in-system way to swap it: the OS-governed swap flow exists in the backend but is unreachable (no path feeds it a diagnosis, so its entry guards can never pass). And even if it executed, custody records would change while actual internet delivery — driven by the old-app Remote and Inventory services — would not, leaving the customer offline with a "completed" swap.
Who experiences it?Customers with device-fault internet issues (no resolution, or a paper resolution with no internet); CSPs and technicians (no legitimate swap path); Wiom inventory & finance (swaps happen off-books and cannot be reconciled).
Cost of leaving it as-is?Every swap remains an unaudited device movement: unrecoverable devices, unreconcilable inventory, customer downtime, and a custody ledger that can drift from connectivity reality.

A3. Current Behaviour WHAT

Confirmed with author 2026-06-06 (re-scoped to swap-only in v0.2). Observable behaviour of the slice being changed. Technical references inline per author directive.
Trigger / conditionObservable behaviour today
CSP works a RESTORE task and concludes the device is faultyThere is no way to tell the system. The diagnosis fields exist on the task record (complaint_tasks.diagnosis_outcome, complaint_tasks.diagnosis_fault_type) but nothing feeds them — so the swap entry guards (G8 "diagnosis complete", G9 "device fault type") can never pass, and the swap flow is unreachable in practice.
A swap is submitted (POST /tasks/{taskId}/device-swap with old_device_id, new_device_id)SRS validates G6–G11 — including G6, a connection-state query to CL OS — and emits DEVICE_SWAP. ACS validates G1–G5 and immediately executes the atomic custody transition (old device DEPLOYED → RETRIEVAL_PENDING, new device CUSTODIED/IDLE → DEPLOYED), then emits DEVICE_SWAP_COMPLETED. No external system is consulted before execution.
The swap completesNothing informs the old-app stack (Remote service, Inventory service) — actual internet delivery does not shift to the new device. Custody says "swapped"; connectivity says otherwise. On receiving DEVICE_SWAP_COMPLETED, SRS records the swap on the complaint (complaints.device_swap_old_device_id, device_swap_new_device_id, device_swap_initiated_at, device_swap_completed_at, resolution_type = DEVICE_REPLACED) and auto-moves the complaint to PENDING_VERIFICATION — swap completion is treated as the resolution action itself.
Verification window runsVerification is SRS-internal today (telemetry check always inconclusive in v1; customer confirmation or timeout resolves; denial re-escalates). A common verification service — which will verify all service tickets in general, not just swaps — is being built in parallel and is not yet the verifier.
CSP attempts another swap on the same complaintAllowed for the time being — guards re-evaluate and a second swap proceeds if they pass. Nothing additional governs it.
Guard failure / no completionAny guard failure → rejection with reason (DEVICE_SWAP_REJECTED), complaint unchanged. No completion within 120 s (P_SWAP_COMPLETION_TIMEOUT) → ops flag, complaint stays open, late completion still processed.

A4. Desired Behaviour WHAT

Trigger / conditionDesired observable behaviour
CSP initiates a device swap on an open service ticketThe backend receives, in the swap-initiation request: the diagnosis fault type, the diagnosis outcome, the resolution type (DEVICE_REPLACED), and the replacement device. The frontend journey is the frontend's decision. SRS persists the diagnosis onto the active task before evaluating guards.
Gate checks runSRS validates its guards G7–G11 (open complaint; diagnosis present; device fault type; replacement specified; resolution type). G6 is removed — no connection-state query. ACS validates its guards G1–G5 (old device DEPLOYED & bound to this connection; new device CUSTODIED/IDLE; same CSP) without executing yet. Any failure → CSP shown the reason; nothing changes.
Gate checks pass → SRS shifts connectivitySRS (not ACS) calls the Remote service and Inventory service to execute the connectivity shift onto the new device. (Exact operations: TBD with Rahul Nijhawan.)
SRS asks the Validation Layer to confirm the swap really happenedSRS calls the Validation Layer (VL) — the same common verification layer being built for all ticket types. For a swap, VL confirms via router pings: the new device has pinged at least once in the last 10 minutes and the old device is no longer pinging. If not yet satisfied, VL retries (up to 2 retries, 20 minutes total) on the normal ~5-minute ping cadence. The CSP sees a synchronous "verifying…" screen with a countdown.
VL = CONFIRMEDSRS signals ACS to execute the atomic custody transition (old device DEPLOYED → RETRIEVAL_PENDING, new device CUSTODIED/IDLE → DEPLOYED); ACS emits DEVICE_SWAP_COMPLETED back to SRS. SRS updates its own device mapping to the new device and auto-closes the ticket — a confirmed swap is the resolution.
VL = DECLINED or NO_DATASRS reverses the connectivity shift by re-calling Remote + Inventory to restore the pre-swap state. ACS never executed, so there is nothing to undo in custody. The ticket stays open; the validation outcome is flagged for analysis; the CSP is shown: "We couldn't verify the swap. If you've found another problem, fix it and retry; otherwise swap the devices back (old → customer, new → CSP)." No physical return is needed unless they give up.
Ticket reopens after a confirmed+closed swap (within reopen window)Same complaint lineage, no fresh SLA clock — and the reopened ticket runs against the new device, because the swap already updated the device facts on completion.
Lifecycle boundariesA swap can only start on an open, actionable ticket. swap_reason is always FAULTY_DEVICE in v1; UPGRADE remains a declared placeholder.

A6. Must-Not-Change — Regression Guard GUARD

#Adjacent behaviour that must not changeWhy it's in the blast radius
G-ANon-swap resolutions on a RESTORE task behave exactly as before — same states, same flow into verification. Diagnosis is not mandatory for them.D2 adds diagnosis to the swap path only; it must not become a gate for other resolutions.
G-BThe atomic custody transition itself (old → RETRIEVAL_PENDING, new → DEPLOYED), guards G1–G5, the DEVICE_SWAP_COMPLETED payload, and event idempotency are unchanged. The validate/execute split (D5) changes when ACS acts, not how.Splitting ACS into validate + execute could tempt a behaviour change in the transition — forbidden.
G-CA swap never changes connection state and emits no signal to CL OS, Compensation OS, or Quality OS by itself.D1 removes the only CL interaction; nothing must creep in to replace it.
G-DCOMPLAINT_RESOLUTION_SIGNAL to Quality OS keeps its schema and semantics. A swap-closed ticket emits the same signal as any other closure.D7 makes a swap auto-close the ticket; the closure signal itself must stay byte-identical.
G-EReclassification-to-platform (router fault, system-verifiable evidence, time-bounded) unchanged.Sibling device-fault path; the swap diagnosis must not substitute for its evidence.
G-FNormal installation binding (CUSTODIED → DEPLOYED) unchanged. The old-app shift, VL gate, and reversal apply only to swaps.Shares the custody machine; the new steps must not leak into installs.
G-GVerification is the common verification layer's responsibility. This change feeds it swap-specific inputs (the ping rules) but does not fork it into a separate swap-only verifier.D4 routes swaps through the common layer; building a parallel verifier would split the verification truth.
G-HSwap-completion timeout semantics: ops flag on expiry, ticket not auto-resolved on timeout, late completion still processed.D4/D5 lengthen the path (old-app + 20-min VL window); the timeout value changes (B3) but its semantics must survive.

A7. Rules, Invariants & Edge Cases Affected WHAT

A7.1 Rules & invariants (must always hold)

#Rule / invariantStatement
I1INV — complaint anchoringEvery swap initiation carries a complaint_id whose complaint is in ASSIGNED or ESCALATED at initiation. A swap without an open ticket is structurally impossible.
I2INV — custody atomicityWhen ACS executes, either both devices transition (old → RETRIEVAL_PENDING and new → DEPLOYED) or neither does. No partial custody outcome is ever observable.
I3INV — single bindingA connection has at most one device bound to it at any moment, including across repeat swaps. After a confirmed swap, exactly the new device is bound.
I4INV — diagnosis gateNo swap proceeds unless the initiation carried a fault type in {DEVICE_HARDWARE, DEVICE_SOFTWARE, DEVICE_CONNECTIVITY}, persisted on the task before guard evaluation. Latest diagnosis governs.
I5INV — audit completenessEvery completed swap is recorded on its complaint with old device id, new device id, initiation + completion timestamps, and swap_validation_outcome — reconcilable from complaint records alone, including multiple swaps per complaint.
I6INV — verify-before-commitACS commits the custody transition only after VL = CONFIRMED. A DECLINED or NO_DATA outcome coexists with zero custody change and a reversal of the old-app shift — custody never claims a swap the network did not perform.
I7INV — resolution-type consistencyEvery swap initiation carries, and every completed swap records, resolution_type = DEVICE_REPLACED. No completed swap has a missing or different resolution type.
I8INV — no false closureA swap auto-closes the ticket only on VL = CONFIRMED. DECLINED and NO_DATA never close the ticket — they reverse the shift and leave the ticket open.
R1RULE — unlimited repeat swapsAny number of swaps may run against one ticket; each re-runs the full flow (gate → shift → VL → commit). No cap parameter in v1.
R2RULE — swap-as-closureA VL-confirmed swap closes the ticket. There is no separate resolution submission for a swap-resolved ticket.
R3RULE — duplicate-submission safetyWhile a swap is in flight, a second initiation for the same task is rejected as "swap in progress". A duplicate of an already-processed custody execution produces no second transition (idempotent).
R4RULE — no un-swap of a confirmed swapOnce a swap is confirmed and the ticket closed, the custody change is never reversed. Any later need is a fresh forward swap on a new (reopened) ticket.

B2. State Changes Strict conditional

No state is added or removed. The complaint state machine gains an automatic closure path on confirmed swap; the custody machine is unchanged (its swap transitions exist; only their trigger is gated on VL).
State machineChange
Complaint (SRS)A VL-confirmed swap completion drives the ticket to closure automatically (a confirmed swap is the resolution — D7/R2). A DECLINED/NO_DATA swap causes no state change (ticket stays in ASSIGNED/ESCALATED). No new state is introduced.
Device custody (ACS)Untouched states and transitions. The only change is when the transition fires: on SRS's VL-confirmed signal, not immediately on guard pass (D5).

F. Future Scope — Noted, Not Specced Out of scope for this version

Items intentionally parked. Recorded so the intent isn't lost, but explicitly not part of this build. Each needs its own spec (and, where flagged, its own cross-OS amendments) before it can be picked up.

F1. Closing a Pickup Ticket (PUT) by swapping a device

AspectDetail
Today's scopeThis spec enables a swap only when the customer has raised a service ticket. The swap lives inside that complaint's lifecycle.
The gap / scenarioFrustrated customers often raise a Pickup Ticket (PUT) — they want to leave Wiom and return the device. The CSP visits, diagnoses on site, and convinces the customer to stay on the condition that a new device is provided. Today a PUT can only close by the customer recharging or by the device being picked up — neither reflects a save-via-swap.
The proposalIntroduce swapping as a third PUT-closure path, alongside recharge and pickup. A PUT would close as "retained via device replacement", old device routes to return-to-Wiom, new device binds to the connection.
Why deferredMaterially larger than a service-ticket swap: it changes how a PUT terminates, rippling across Exit OS (PUT lifecycle), CL OS (a connection saved from exit), Asset Custody OS (swap from a non-complaint context), and SR OS. It also breaks this spec's invariant I1 ("every swap carries an open complaint_id"), since a PUT-anchored swap has no complaint. Its own initiative.
StatusNoted only. Separate change spec when prioritised.

F2. Device locking / concurrency between gate-check and execution

AspectDetail
The gapGate checks (G1–G5) run at initiation, but ACS executes custody up to ~20 min later (after the VL window). In that gap, a device's state could change underneath the swap (e.g., deployed elsewhere, deactivated), so a stale validation could execute.
Why deferredJudged too small an edge for this effort; a full fix needs reservation/lock checks across multiple OSes and services. Accepted as a known limitation for v1 — ACS re-reads device state at execution, but no exclusive lock is held.
StatusNoted only. Revisit if it shows up in practice.

F3. Validation beyond a ping (true service check)

AspectDetail
The gapv1 treats a router ping as proof of internet. A device can ping while the customer still has no usable service (auth/plan/throughput issues).
Why deferredDeeper checks (session/auth/throughput) are a future enhancement to the validation layer. For v1, ping = internet, with the swap_validation_outcome flag available to spot patterns that justify adding layers.
StatusNoted only. Future VL enhancement.
