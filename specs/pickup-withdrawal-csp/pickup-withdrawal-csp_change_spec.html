Customer-Initiated Pickup Withdrawal — CSP Side

A customer who raised a netbox pickup request by mistake has no way to take it back — their only route today is to pay for another recharge. This spec adds a withdrawal path on the CSP side: a customer whose plan is still live can cancel their own pickup request, and the CSP world unwinds exactly as it does when a customer recharges. The customer-app trigger and its screens are specified separately.

Author: Jaivin Prajapati (PM)  ·  Reviewer: CSP Engineering  ·  Tier: Full
Scope fence: this spec covers the CSP side only — what the CSP world must do to accept and act on a withdrawal signal. The customer-app trigger, screens, eligibility messaging and the pre-check call are specified in the companion Customer-Initiated Pickup Withdrawal — Customer Side spec.
Blocking prerequisite: defect C1 (see Blocking prerequisite) must ship before this change. Build must not start until C1 is merged.

A2. Why This Change WHAT

QuestionAnswer
What's wrong with the current behaviour?A netbox pickup request raised by the customer cannot be taken back. Once raised, the pickup can only end three ways: the CSP collects the device, the customer pays for a fresh recharge, or the task expires. A customer who tapped it by mistake, or changed their mind an hour later, has no way to say so — even though their plan is still live and paid.
Who experiences it?Customers who raise a pickup request while their plan is still active — mistaken taps, exploratory taps, and genuine changes of mind. Downstream, the CSP is sent to collect a device the customer intends to keep.
Cost of leaving it as-is?The customer is pushed into paying for a recharge they do not need, or loses a service they wanted to keep. The CSP makes a wasted trip. Both outcomes are avoidable, and both are read by the customer as the product refusing to listen.

A3. Current Behaviour WHAT

What is expected: the observable status quo for the slice about to change.
Trigger / conditionObservable behaviour today
Customer raises a pickup request from the customer app, plan still liveThe connection moves into a pending-deactivation state, the customer's device is marked as awaiting recovery from the customer, and a netbox pickup task appears in the CSP's task feed. The customer keeps service until the plan lapses.
Plan lapses beyond the deactivation threshold with no rechargeThe system raises the same pickup automatically. Indistinguishable to the CSP from a customer-raised pickup.
Customer changes their mind and wants to keep the serviceNo withdrawal option exists. The only way to stop the pickup is to buy a fresh recharge, which cancels the task as a side effect.
Customer recharges while the pickup is openThe pickup task is cancelled, the device is restored to deployed, and the connection returns to active or paused depending on whether the ISP recharge window is still open. The customer keeps the device.
CSP collects the device, or the task expiresThe pickup reaches its terminal state and the customer's service ends.

A4. Desired Behaviour WHAT

Trigger / conditionDesired observable behaviour
Customer withdraws a pickup they raised themselves, while their plan is live and the pickup is still openThe pickup task is cancelled, the device returns to deployed with the customer, and the connection returns to active or paused on exactly the same recharge-window rule the recharge path uses. The customer keeps the device and the service, and pays nothing.
Customer attempts to withdraw a pickup the system raised (plan lapsed past threshold)Withdrawal is refused. The customer is told a recharge is required — the existing recharge path remains their route.
Customer attempts to withdraw after their plan has lapsedWithdrawal is refused. A recharge is required.
Customer attempts to withdraw a pickup that has already reached a terminal stateWithdrawal is refused, with the reason distinguishing "already collected" from "already cancelled" and from "expired".
Customer withdraws while the CSP has the pickup in progressWithdrawal succeeds. The task is cancelled from whatever active state it was in, matching how a recharge behaves today.
Customer recharges while the pickup is openUnchanged in every respect.

A5. The Delta WHAT

What is expected: one atomic change per row. Each row is buildable and testable on its own.
What changesBeforeAfterWhy
D1. Withdrawal entry point No way to withdraw a pickup. Only a recharge, a collection, or expiry ends it. A customer-app-sourced withdrawal signal is accepted by the connection lifecycle and drives the same unwind the recharge path drives. Gives the customer a way to undo a mistaken pickup without paying.
D2. Eligibility — origin of the pickup N/A — nothing to be eligible for. Withdrawal is permitted only when the pending deactivation was customer-initiated. System-initiated deactivations (plan lapsed past threshold) are refused. A customer cannot withdraw their way out of a lapsed plan; only a payment resolves that.
D3. Eligibility — plan must be live N/A. Withdrawal is permitted only when the customer's entitlement end is in the future at the moment of the request. Withdrawal restores a service the customer has already paid for. With no live entitlement there is nothing to restore.
D4. Eligibility — pickup must be open N/A. Withdrawal is permitted only when the pickup task is still in an active (non-terminal) state. A collected, cancelled or expired pickup cannot be unwound.
D5. Which active pickup states allow withdrawal N/A. All active states, including after the CSP has acknowledged and after proof has been submitted — identical to the recharge path's cut-off. Deliberate: withdrawal must behave exactly as a recharge. The overlap case (CSP claims collection while the customer wants to continue) is vanishingly rare and is not worth a divergent rule.
D6. Connection landing state N/A. Active if the ISP recharge window is still in the future at the moment of withdrawal; paused if it has lapsed. Same rule, same source of truth as the recharge path. Reuses a settled rule rather than inventing a second one that could drift.
D7. Customer access state after withdrawal N/A. Restored to enabled when the connection lands active; blocked-by-supply when it lands paused. Access must match the connection's real state — the customer has a paid, live plan.
D8. Device custody after withdrawal Device stays awaiting-recovery until the pickup terminates. Device returns to deployed against the same connection, and the awaiting-recovery markers are cleared. The device stays with the customer and must stop counting as pending recovery.
D9. Pickup task outcome Cancels only as rescued (recharge), CSP-reported-unable, or exit escalation. Cancels with a new, distinct cancellation reason and reason code meaning "withdrawn by the customer". Recording a withdrawal as a rescue would misreport it as a recharge and make the two permanently indistinguishable in reporting.
D10. Rescue-signal contract The rescue signal implies a recharge and carries a recharge reference. Carries an explicit rescue source distinguishing a recharge from a customer withdrawal; the recharge reference is optional and empty on the withdrawal path. Downstream services must be able to tell the two apart to pick the right reason code and copy.
D11. CSP-facing task copy Cancelled tasks show a reason string; the rescue reason has no CSP-app string and renders as a raw internal key. The new withdrawal reason renders a proper CSP-app string. The existing rescue, exit and failure reasons get their missing strings at the same time. The CSP must be able to read why the task disappeared. Fixing the neighbouring gaps is near-zero cost and prevents another raw key leaking.
D12. Allocation deactivation marker The marker set when deactivation starts is never cleared on a rescue; the allocation reads as permanently mid-deactivation. Cleared whenever a connection is rescued, on both the withdrawal and the recharge path. A stale marker misrepresents live allocations and will mislead any future logic that reads it.
D13. Eligibility pre-check surface No way to ask whether a pickup can be withdrawn; the customer would only discover refusal after attempting. A read-only check reports each eligibility condition (D2, D3, D4) separately, so the customer app can show the right message and hide the action when it cannot succeed. A withdrawal button that fails after the tap is worse than no button. Consumed by the customer-side spec.
D14. CSP notification on withdrawal The CSP is notified when a pickup is assigned, never when it is cancelled. Unchanged — no notification. Explicitly matching the recharge path. Recorded here as a conscious decision, not an oversight; see A7.2 E6 for the residual risk.
D15. CSP compensation on withdrawal No compensation on a recharge-driven cancellation. Unchanged — none. Matching the recharge path. A CSP already en route is an accepted cost, as it is today.

A6. Must-Not-Change — Regression Guard GUARD

What is expected: adjacent behaviours genuinely in the blast radius, each with the reason it is at risk. Every line has a matching REGRESSION block in B5.
Must not changeWhy it is at risk
G1. The recharge-driven cancellation path, end to end — a customer who recharges while a pickup is open still gets the task cancelled, device restored and connection resumed, reported as a rescue.The withdrawal path reuses the same signal and the same handlers. A branch added for withdrawal can silently alter the recharge branch.
G2. System-raised pickups (plan lapsed past threshold) remain rescuable by a recharge.Narrowing the eligible-reason rule for withdrawal could narrow it for the recharge path too, stranding every auto-raised pickup.
G3. The CSP's own "unable to pick up" cancellation, and its restriction to pre-proof states.It shares the pickup task's cancellation machinery and reason-code set.
G4. Exit-driven and device-lost terminations of the pickup task.They share the same terminal-transition path and are easy to break when a new terminal reason is introduced.
G5. The reactive-obligation rule — an obligation is raised only when the connection lands paused and the customer's entitlement is still live.It sits immediately below the state writes this change touches, and reads the same signal.
G6. Normal pickup creation — both customer-raised and system-raised pickups still open a task and mark the device awaiting recovery.The withdrawal path writes to the same connection and device records; a shared guard change could block creation.
G7. The active-connection count held for each CSP stays accurate across a withdraw.The count is decremented when deactivation starts and re-incremented only on a genuine return to active. A withdrawal must not double-count or leak.
G8. Repeat delivery of the same signal changes nothing beyond the first.The unwind spans four services; a partially-idempotent path would produce a half-restored connection.

A7. Rules, Invariants & Edge Cases Affected WHAT

A7.1 Rules & invariants

RefTypeStatement
R1Rule (new)A pickup withdrawal is accepted only when all three hold at the moment of the request: the pending deactivation was customer-initiated; the customer's entitlement end is in the future; the pickup task is in an active state. Failing any one refuses the withdrawal.
R2Rule (new)An accepted withdrawal produces the same end state as a recharge-driven rescue, differing only in the recorded reason.
R3Rule (new)A withdrawal is recorded against the pickup task with a cancellation reason and reason code that identify it as customer-withdrawn, never as rescued.
R4Rule (altered)The set of deactivation reasons eligible for unwinding is now path-dependent: the recharge path keeps both customer-initiated and lapsed-plan reasons; the withdrawal path accepts customer-initiated only.
R5Rule (unchanged, restated)The connection's landing state is decided solely by whether the ISP recharge window is still open, independent of what triggered the unwind.
I1InvariantAfter any completed unwind, the customer's recorded connection state equals the connection lifecycle's actual state for that connection.
I2InvariantA customer whose plan is live and whose connection is active is never left in a blocked access state.
I3InvariantA device is in the awaiting-recovery condition if and only if an active pickup task exists for it.
I4InvariantA pickup task has at most one terminal outcome, and its recorded reason identifies which of the terminating causes applied.
I5InvariantA withdrawal never moves money: no charge, no refund, no CSP payout, no change to any deposit or wallet balance.
I6InvariantThe CSP's active-connection count reflects exactly the connections currently active for that CSP, at every point during and after a withdrawal.

A7.2 Edge cases

RefConditionIntended outcome
E1Customer withdraws, and the CSP marks the device collected moments later.Whichever lands first wins; the second is refused because the task is no longer in an active state. No state is half-applied. The losing actor is told the current state.
E2Customer withdraws while the pickup shows proof submitted or under verification.Withdrawal succeeds (D5). The task cancels as customer-withdrawn and the device returns to deployed. Accepted asymmetry: if the CSP genuinely holds the device, the position is recoverable through the existing device-return route.
E3Customer withdraws and recharges in the same window.Both are unwinds to the same end state. The first to land performs it; the second finds nothing to unwind and completes without effect. The recharge's entitlement extension still applies.
E4Customer's entitlement lapses between the eligibility pre-check and the withdrawal request.Refused at the withdrawal, not at the pre-check. Eligibility is authoritative at the moment of the request, never at the moment of the check.
E5The customer's plan is live but the ISP recharge window has lapsed.Withdrawal is allowed (the plan is live). The connection lands paused, access goes to blocked-by-supply, and the existing reactive-obligation rule fires — identical to a recharge landing in the same position.
E6The CSP is already travelling to collect when the customer withdraws.The task disappears from the CSP's feed with no push notification (D14). Accepted, matching the recharge path. Residual risk, recorded deliberately: the CSP may complete a wasted trip. Revisit if withdrawal volume makes it material.
E7Withdrawal arrives for a connection whose device was already declared lost.Handled by the existing late-recovery route, which already restores a lost device on a rescue signal. No new behaviour; the pickup task cancels as customer-withdrawn.
E8Withdrawal arrives twice, or is retried after a timeout.The first completes the unwind; every repeat completes without further effect and reports success. No duplicate cancellation record, no duplicate state emission.
E9Withdrawal arrives for a connection with no pickup task and no pending deactivation.Refused as ineligible. Nothing is written.
E10The unwind partially fails — for example the pickup task cancels but the device restore does not.The device is the binding record. An unwind that has not restored the device must be retryable to completion, and must not leave the customer active with the device still marked for recovery. Recovery mechanism is engineering's.

B5. Acceptance Criteria — Verification Layer HOW

What is expected: exhaustive coverage of five classes. Every block is labelled so coverage can be audited.

Business logic

LOGIC-01 — happy path (D1, R1, R2)
Given a customer raised a pickup themselves, their plan is live, the ISP recharge window is still open, and the pickup task is active
When the customer withdraws the pickup
Then the pickup task is cancelled, the device returns to deployed against the same connection, the connection becomes active and the customer's access is enabled.
LOGIC-02 — refused, system-raised pickup (D2, R1, R4)
Given the pending deactivation was raised by the system because the plan lapsed past the threshold
When the customer attempts to withdraw
Then the withdrawal is refused, nothing is written, and the reason returned identifies the pickup as system-raised.
LOGIC-03 — refused, plan not live (D3, R1)
Given the pickup was customer-raised but the customer's entitlement has lapsed
When the customer attempts to withdraw
Then the withdrawal is refused, nothing is written, and the reason identifies the lapsed plan.
LOGIC-04 — refused, pickup already terminal (D4, R1)
Given the pickup task has already been completed, cancelled or expired
When the customer attempts to withdraw
Then the withdrawal is refused, nothing is written, and the reason distinguishes completed from cancelled from expired.
LOGIC-05 — withdrawal from an acknowledged pickup (D5, E2)
Given the CSP has acknowledged the pickup and it is in progress
When the customer withdraws
Then the withdrawal succeeds and the task cancels as customer-withdrawn.
LOGIC-06 — withdrawal after proof submitted (D5, E2)
Given the CSP has submitted collection proof and it is under verification
When the customer withdraws
Then the withdrawal still succeeds and the task cancels as customer-withdrawn — matching the recharge path exactly.
LOGIC-07 — recorded reason (D9, R3)
Given a withdrawal has completed
When the cancelled pickup is inspected
Then its cancellation reason and reason code identify it as customer-withdrawn, and never as rescued.
LOGIC-08 — eligibility pre-check reports conditions separately (D13)
Given a connection failing two of the three eligibility conditions
When the pre-check is called
Then it reports each condition's pass/fail separately, rather than a single combined verdict.
LOGIC-09 — allocation marker cleared (D12)
Given a connection whose deactivation marker was set when the pickup was raised
When a withdrawal completes
Then the marker is cleared and the allocation no longer reads as mid-deactivation.
LOGIC-10 — no money moves (I5, D15)
Given any completed withdrawal
When all financial records for the customer and the CSP are inspected
Then no charge, refund, payout, deposit or wallet movement has occurred.

Computation & calculation

COMPUTE-01 — landing state, window open (D6, R5)
Given the ISP recharge window ends in the future
When a withdrawal is accepted
Then the connection lands active.
COMPUTE-02 — landing state, window lapsed (D6, R5, E5)
Given the ISP recharge window ended in the past while the customer's plan is still live
When a withdrawal is accepted
Then the connection lands paused, access goes to blocked-by-supply, and the existing reactive obligation is raised.
COMPUTE-03 — boundary, window ends exactly now (D6, R5)
Given the ISP recharge window end equals the instant of the withdrawal
When the withdrawal is accepted
Then the connection lands paused — an end instant that is not strictly in the future counts as lapsed, matching the recharge path exactly.
COMPUTE-04 — boundary, no window on record (D6, R5)
Given no ISP recharge window is recorded for the connection
When a withdrawal is accepted
Then the connection lands paused, matching the recharge path's treatment of an absent window.
COMPUTE-05 — boundary, entitlement ends exactly now (D3, E4)
Given the customer's entitlement end equals the instant of the withdrawal request
When the withdrawal is attempted
Then it is refused — the entitlement must be strictly in the future to qualify as live.
COMPUTE-06 — boundary, eligibility evaluated at request time not check time (E4)
Given the pre-check passed while the plan was live, and the plan lapses before the withdrawal arrives
When the withdrawal is attempted
Then it is refused on the state at the moment of the request.

Invariants

INVARIANT-01 (I1)
Given any path through a completed unwind — withdrawal or recharge, landing active or paused, first delivery or replay
Then the customer's recorded connection state always equals the connection lifecycle's actual state for that connection.
INVARIANT-02 (I2)
Given any moment after a completed unwind
Then a customer with a live plan on an active connection is never in a blocked access state.
INVARIANT-03 (I3)
Given any moment, including mid-unwind
Then a device is marked awaiting recovery if and only if an active pickup task exists for it.
INVARIANT-04 (I4)
Given any pickup task that has terminated
Then it has exactly one terminal outcome, and its recorded reason identifies which cause applied.
INVARIANT-05 (I5)
Given any path through a withdrawal, including refused and partially-failed attempts
Then no financial record for the customer or the CSP has changed.
INVARIANT-06 (I6)
Given any point during or after a withdrawal, including replays
Then the CSP's active-connection count equals the number of connections actually active for that CSP.

Use & edge cases

— Blocking Prerequisite: defect C1 BLOCKER

Status: this change must not start build until C1 is merged. Filed separately as C1 — CAEO rescue state defect and routed to the CAEO owner.

What is broken

When a connection is rescued out of pending deactivation, the customer-access record is written as paused / blocked-by-supply regardless of where the connection actually landed. The rescue signal carries the landing state, but only the reactive-obligation rule reads it; the two state writes above it do not.

Why it blocks this change specifically

Withdrawal eligibility requires a live plan (D3), so an accepted withdrawal always lands the connection active. Every single withdrawal would therefore hit the broken branch: the customer would withdraw their pickup, keep their device — and be left access-blocked on a plan they have already paid for. That is a worse outcome than the gap this spec sets out to close.

Why it is filed separately

The defect is pre-existing and already reachable on the live recharge path whenever a customer recharges while their ISP window is still open. It is not caused by this change and should be fixed on its own timeline. It also silently re-creates the customer-access state drift that required a manual correction of several thousand records in June 2026 — so it warrants attention independent of whether this feature ships.

The fix, in business terms

When the connection landsAccess state todayAccess state required
Active — ISP window still openBlocked by supply, with a supply-disruption marker setEnabled, no supply-disruption marker
Paused — ISP window lapsedBlocked by supplyBlocked by supply — unchanged

The reactive-obligation rule sitting below these writes is correct as it stands and must not be touched: an obligation is raised only when the connection lands paused and the customer's entitlement is still live. The C1 note carries the code-level location, the proposed patch and its own test cases.
